Cognitive dissonance as friction

I experience a lot of cognitive dissonance in being a user experience designer and possessing the belief that we must all live with a bit more friction in our personal lives. It is an inherent part of my job to create as close to a seamless experience as possible, and any suspicion of friction must be squashed like the spotted lanternfly.

Recently, I’ve struggled articulating why I feel so uninspired by my work and pinpointing the origins of this dissonance. What used to feel like innovation’s frontier now feels like a cycle of eternally meaningless production.

Underneath, my beliefs coalesced like oil in water - a lava lamp with orbs floating stale and contained. Attempting to stay afloat amongst a rapidly changing industry, these orbs could only be fragmented into teenier, tinier spheres, never fully dissolved or integrated.

The demand to bring more friction into my life directly contradicts my professional career, one that I do truly enjoy. It is hard to feel creative and in love with my work when it revolves around reducing experiences to their most elementary level or modeling designs after already existing ones.

But I often forget that friction exists on a spectrum of intentional and needless, and the near invisible line that separates the two ultimately led me to mistake seamlessness as something to be desperately pursued in everyday life. For a long time, I was certain that seeking out the effortless path was the ideal approach to life. I’d be able to move faster! Extend myself further! Produce more!

And yet, the feeling of fulfillment was shrinking. With everything at my fingertips, I lost opportunities to connect, critique, and question. Everything came so easily, which was only exacerbated by the rise of artificial intelligence. I was addicted to instant gratification.

And so, the journey to introduce more friction into my life began. Small steps like taking the stairs when there is the choice of an escalator and picking up fiber arts, a notoriously finicky and unforgiving craft. None of these things are radical in the slightest. They can be easily sought out. But I wore the dopamine rush like a wool sweater, and what had initially swaddled me with soft comfort became irritatingly hot and itchy.

In its most basic form, friction is an encounter between two things. It is a resistance, perhaps against the status quo. It generates warmth in your hands. It melts the pain of a stricken area. It erodes the jagged edges of a rock that would otherwise pierce your skin. It keeps you from slipping off the face of the earth.

There is no resolution to this cognitive dissonance, and there is no desire for congruency because dissonance is friction. The contact of my inner and outer personas against each other allow me to build meaningful connections and stay curious of the world around me. I can only hope that they might join with one another and float in unity.
